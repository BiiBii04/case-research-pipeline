# Brand Communication Case — Checklist and Framework

This skill turns a case-solving method into something usable live, during a competition, under time pressure. It is one member of a family of case-type skills (`case-brand-communication`, `case-profit-optimization`, and later `case-market-entry`, `case-ma`, and others) that together cover the case types a team might draw. Read the "How this fits with other case-type skills" section below first if more than one case-type skill is loaded — it explains how to keep the final answer coherent instead of stapling together separate mini-answers.

The content here is an original, rewritten summary of public-domain marketing models (SCQ, 3Cs, IMC, ART, etc.). Keep this skill team-internal; don't redistribute outside the competition team.

**By design, this skill is company-agnostic.** It knows how to recognise a brand-communication case and how to solve one — it does not know anything about any specific company, and doesn't reference any particular team's research files by name. That's deliberate: the checklist and framework need to work unchanged on whatever brief actually drops, for this competition or a future one, without being tuned (or biased) toward one team's prep material. If your team has built company-specific research for the current competition, keep the pointer to it in a separate, clearly-labelled file outside this skill (see Section 5) — don't fold it into this skill's own content.

## 1. Where "Brand Communication" sits among case types

A 48-hour business case can land in several different problem families. Brand communication is specifically about changing what a target audience *thinks, feels, or does* toward a brand through message and media — not about changing the product, price, channel structure, or org chart.

| Case type | Core question | Primary tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand Communication** | How do we change perception/behaviour toward a brand through a campaign or message? | Issue → Objective → SoG → Winning Point → J2BD → Insight → Idea → IMC → Trade → KPI |
| Market Entry / Expansion | Should we enter a new market/segment/format, and how? | TAM/SAM/SOM, entry-mode analysis, go/no-go criteria |
| Growth Strategy | Where should the next unit of growth come from? | Ansoff matrix, portfolio/SoG-style analysis |
| Turnaround / Crisis | How do we stop the bleeding and stabilise after a shock? | Root-cause diagnosis, stakeholder triage, phased plan (comms may be one workstream, not the whole answer) |
| Pricing | Is the price right, and how should it change? | Elasticity, competitor benchmarking, value-based pricing |
| Operations / Supply Chain | How do we fix a cost, availability, or efficiency problem? | Process mapping, cost-driver analysis |
| M&A / Partnership | Should we buy, merge with, or partner with another player? | Synergy analysis, valuation, integration risk |

A live brief is rarely a pure type. "Declining store footfall" could be a brand-communication problem (awareness/perception), a pricing problem, or an operations problem (store experience), depending on the actual root cause. The checklist below is how the team tells which one it's actually holding.

## 2. Checklist — is this a brand communication case?

Run this in the first 15-20 minutes after the brief drops, out loud, as a group. Score it loosely — the more left-column boxes get ticked, the more confidently the team can commit to this framework.

**Signals it IS brand communication**
- The brief centres on awareness, perception, image, trust, or sentiment toward a specific brand or sub-brand — not the product spec, price, or distribution network itself.
- The ask is to build, fix, relaunch, or defend a campaign, message, positioning, or communication plan.
- The complication is a gap between reality and perception: "the product/service is strong but nobody knows/believes it," or "a competitor is winning the conversation even though we're comparable on product."
- There's a specific, nameable target audience whose mind needs to change — not a structural/operational fix.
- The brief mentions or implies media, channels, KOLs/influencers, social platforms, PR, advertising, or in-store communication materials.
- The deliverable it's fishing for sounds like a campaign idea, key message, or IMC plan — not a financial model, an operating plan, or an org chart.
- A launch or relaunch has underperformed specifically on awareness/trial (not distribution, price, or product quality).

**Signals it's something else (redirect to the matching skill)**
- Root complaint is price, margin, or cost structure → Pricing/Operations; comms is at most a supporting workstream.
- "Should we open in province X / launch format Y" → Market Entry; use TAM/SAM/SOM and entry-mode logic as the spine, not IMC.
- Financial collapse, executive scandal, supply failure, regulatory shutdown → Turnaround/Crisis; a comms plan may be one component, not the primary deliverable.
- Store layout, staffing, logistics, stockouts → Operations.
- Acquiring, partnering, or divesting a business unit → M&A.

**When it's mixed:** many real prompts blend types — e.g. a turnaround case where rebuilding trust is one lever among several. Scope this framework to the specific sub-question that's actually about perception/message, say explicitly which parts of the case use which framework, and don't force the whole IMC machinery onto a case that's 80% operational.

## 3. How this fits with other case-type skills

**If `case-orchestrator` is loaded, it now owns this logic centrally.** It reads the whole brief, segments it into sub-questions, classifies each one against every case-type skill's checklist, and integrates every sub-framework's output into one coherent case structure tied to a shared objective. Load `case-orchestrator` first and let it hand off to this skill for any confirmed brand-communication sub-question, rather than re-deriving the classification here. The rest of this section is this skill's own standalone fallback logic, for the case `case-orchestrator` isn't loaded and this skill is being used on its own.

The plan is for this skill to sit alongside siblings for other case types (profit optimization, market entry, M&A, pricing, turnaround, etc.), each with its own checklist and framework. When more than one is available:

1. **Run every available checklist against the brief before committing to one.** Real briefs blend types more often than they're pure.
2. **If exactly one lens fires strongly**, use that skill's framework as the backbone of the whole answer.
3. **If more than one lens fires (a mixed case)**, don't let each framework run in isolation and get stapled together at the end. Nominate one owner for the shared throughline — one SCQ (Situation-Complication-Question) and one overarching Objective that every sub-framework's output has to tie back to. Each specialized section should open by naming which sub-question it's answering and close by connecting back to that shared Objective, so the final answer reads as one solution arrived at from multiple angles, not several disconnected mini-decks.
4. **If nothing fires clearly**, don't force this framework. Say so, and fall back to general business-case structuring (root cause → objective → options → recommendation) rather than distorting the case to fit a template that doesn't match.

**Specifically with `case-profit-optimization`:** the two skills share the same SCQ framing tool for restating the problem, which makes handoff easy — but they diverge immediately after that. A brand-communication sub-question decomposes into a people/perception chain (Issue → Objective → Insight → Idea); a profit-optimization sub-question decomposes into a numbers tree (Revenue/Cost). A brief that's "profit is falling because a campaign underperformed on trial" is a brand-communication case whose success metric happens to be phrased in profit terms — don't mistake the phrasing for the case type. Conversely, a brief that's "should we approve this marketing spend on a cost-benefit basis" is a profit-optimization case that happens to mention marketing — the deliverable is a cost-benefit number, not a campaign.

As sibling skills get added, keep them in the same `case-<type>` naming pattern (e.g. `case-market-entry`, `case-ma`) so they're easy to spot as one family.

## 4. The framework, step by step

The roadmap below is a summary. This public copy does not include the full worked framework detail, formulas, or worked examples (kept out deliberately, see the repo README) — read it as a checklist and step outline, not a complete how-to.

**A. Strategy** — 1. Identify Brand Issues (root cause, via SCQ) → 2. Issue to Objective (Business/Marketing/Communication) → 3. Source of Growth (New Users vs. Loyal Users) → 4. Winning Point (3Cs) → 5. Job To Be Done (Get whom / to do what / by what)

**B. Deployment** — 6. Insight (4-step deep-dive) → 7. Idea (Task Map → Big/Campaign/Execution Idea, judged by A-R-T) → 8. IMC Plan (6 parts, phased Awareness→Consideration→Conversion→Loyalty) → 9. Trade/Shopper Plan (channel-specific in-store execution) → 10. KPI & Budget (top-down or bottom-up)

Each step depends on the one before it — don't skip ahead to Idea before Insight is actually derived, and don't assert a Winning Point without showing the 3Cs Venn logic behind it.

Worked examples are not included in this public copy.

## 5. What information each step needs

If the checklist confirms brand communication (in full or in part), here's what the framework asks for at each step. This list is deliberately generic — it names the *kind* of data each step needs, not a specific team's files, so it stays valid regardless of which company or competition it's applied to.

| Framework step | Data it needs |
|---|---|
| Issue ID — External Drivers | Category trends, market size/growth, competitor activity, consumer trends |
| Issue ID — Internal Drivers | The brand's P&L trajectory, root strengths, communication history — not pre-built; case-specific, comes from the brief/exhibits on the day |
| Source of Growth | Brand's current share/position; size and reachability of candidate segments |
| Winning Point (3Cs) | Consumer needs, competitor strengths/weaknesses, company USP |
| IMC / Trade Plan | Channel structure (modern vs. traditional trade, e-commerce share), shopper profile by channel |
| Regulatory check on any campaign idea | Foreign-retail rules, packaging/sustainability obligations, import rules if the idea touches new SKUs/formats/stores |
| Big Idea / Insight | Persona detail, category-specific consumer truths — not pre-built; build live from the case's actual target segment using the Task Map |
| KPI & Budget | Category revenue benchmark (top-down), historical cost-per-metric data (bottom-up) |

**Before the clock starts on an actual case, check your own team's prep against this list** — for each row, do you already have this data, or will it have to come live from the brief? Two rows (Internal Drivers, Big Idea/Insight) are *never* pre-buildable — they only exist once the real brief lands — so don't burn prep time trying to guess them in advance. Everything else is fair game to research ahead of time.

If your team keeps a research index for the current competition (which company, which files, which framework step each one covers), treat it as a separate document alongside this skill, not part of it — that keeps this skill reusable for a future competition without editing, and keeps this skill's *classification logic* independent of any one team's research, which may be incomplete or unverified in places.

## 6. The case-solving process, if it's brand communication

A suggested run of show once the checklist confirms this case type and the clock starts. Rescale the timing to the actual deadline, but keep the order — each step depends on the one before it.

| Time (of ~10 working hrs) | Step | What the team does |
|---|---|---|
| First 20 min | Confirm case type | Run the Section 2 checklist as a group. Agree explicitly: "this is a brand communication case, scoped to [X]." Assign roles (Issue/Research lead, Insight/Idea lead, IMC/Trade lead, Numbers/KPI lead). |
| 20-90 min | Issue → Objective | Write the SCQ. Pull External Drivers from the prepped research; extract Internal Drivers from the case exhibits. Draft Business/Marketing/Communication objectives — check whether the brief already states them. |
| 90-150 min | SoG → Winning Point | Brand-audit the subject to pick New Users vs. Loyal Users. Build the 3Cs Venn using the prepped Competitor Matrix and Market Share data. Land on ONE winning point. |
| 150-180 min | J2BD | Write the one-liner: Get whom / to do what / by what. Check it references both the SoG and the winning point. |
| 180-270 min | Insight | Build the persona. Run the 4-step deep-dive or the Truth+Tension+Motivation shortcut. Check the insight against all five criteria before moving on. |
| 270-360 min | Idea | Run the Task Map. Draft Big Idea → Campaign Idea → Execution Idea. Judge candidates with A-R-T and the three message checks. |
| 360-480 min | IMC Plan | Fill the 6-part IMC table. Decide phasing. Name one Key Hook per phase. |
| 480-570 min | Trade / Shopper Plan | Pick GT/MT/e-commerce mix from the prepped channel table. Build Pre-Purchase / In-store Visibility / In-store Engagement tactics. |
| 570-630 min | KPI & Budget | Pick top-down or bottom-up (or both, cross-checked). Tie every budget line to a KPI. Flag assumed numbers explicitly. |
| Remaining time | Synthesis & rehearsal | Compress onto one cheat-sheet page. Rehearse the arc: Issue → Objective → SoG → Winning Point → J2BD → Insight → Big Idea → Plan → Numbers. Time-box Q&A prep — expect challenges on SoG sizing and budget assumptions first. |

## 7. Common failure modes to avoid

- **Conflating SoG and Objective.** The objective is what you want to achieve; SoG is who will achieve it for you. Keep them as separate steps.
- **Skipping the 3Cs and just asserting a winning point.** Graders can tell when it wasn't actually derived from a competitor comparison — always show the Venn logic.
- **A double-headed Big Idea.** If the key message needs "and" to explain it, it's not single-minded yet.
- **An IMC plan with no Trade/shopper component.** For a retail business, in-store execution is often more decisive than above-the-line media — don't stop at the campaign concept.
- **Budget numbers with no KPI attached, or KPIs with no cost logic.** Every budget line should trace to a metric, and every metric should trace to a cost.
- **Over-citing market-size numbers once past Objective-setting.** TAM/SAM/SOM belongs in Issue Identification — repeating it in the IMC/Trade sections reads as padding, not analysis.

## 8. One-page decision flow (first few minutes)

1. Read the brief once, cover to cover, before discussing — don't let the first person's guess anchor the room.
2. Underline every noun that names a target audience, and every verb that implies a change in mind/behaviour ("perceive," "trust," "aware," "believe," "choose," "switch") vs. a structural change ("expand," "restructure," "price," "source," "acquire").
3. Score the case against the Section 2 checklist as a group — out loud, fast, don't overthink individual boxes.
4. If it's brand communication: open your team's prepped research (see Section 5), assign roles, start the Section 6 timeline.
5. If it's mixed: agree which sub-question gets this treatment and which gets a different framework (see Section 3), and say so explicitly in the final answer.
6. If it's clearly something else entirely: set this skill aside and use the matching case-type skill instead.
