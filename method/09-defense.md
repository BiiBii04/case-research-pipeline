# Defend

## Purpose

Prepare to defend the recommendation in front of people whose job is to attack it.

The audience varies. A judging panel, a client's leadership team, a board, an interviewer. The preparation does not change much: know where you are weakest, know it before they do, and have an answer that does not require thinking on your feet.

This stage is cheap to do well, because the pipeline has been collecting ammunition the whole way. Every gate logged a tension. Every scored option carries an attack note. Every killed explanation has a documented reason. Most of the work here is harvesting that trail and rehearsing it, plus hunting the questions you did not think of.

**Inputs:** `06-decision.md`, `05-solutions/scored-shortlist.md`, `04-root-cause/synthesis.md`, and the Open tensions in `00-state.md`.

## 1. Harvest what you already know is soft

Pull into one place:

- Every Open tension from `00-state.md`.
- The lowest-scoring dimension of the chosen solution, and why it scored there.
- Every plausible alternative you rejected, and the dimension it lost on.
- Every claim still carrying a low confidence tag or an unresolved conflict.

These are not weaknesses to hide. They are the exact places the questions will land, and you already know all of them, which is an advantage almost nobody in the room has. A team that names its own soft spot before it gets named for them reads as in command of the work.

## 2. Generate the hostile questions

Draft what a sharp, skeptical audience asks. Cover these angles at minimum:

- Why not the obvious solution, or the alternative you rejected?
- Is your root cause really the cause, or a symptom?
- Is this feasible under the client's real constraints, not in theory?
- What is your evidence for the load-bearing claim, and how good is that source?
- Where did that number come from, and is it group or country, this year or last?
- What about the stakeholder who loses from this?
- What is the first thing you would do on Monday, and what does it cost?

Then run an adversary agent in fresh context to generate the ones you did not think of. Fresh context matters. An agent that helped build the recommendation is blind to its assumptions in the same way you are.

## 3. Write the honest best answer to each

For every question, write the strongest answer that is true.

Where the answer is genuinely weak, do not paper over it. Two options, and both are better than bluffing:

- **Trigger a gap-fill wave** to go get the missing evidence, if there is time.
- **Prepare a concession** that holds the line on the core and gives ground at the edge. A concession delivered on your own terms lands very differently from one extracted under fire.

Mark every weak answer as weak in the file, so you know which ones you are hoping not to get.

## 4. State what would change your mind

For the recommendation, name the evidence or the condition that would make you drop it.

This is the part people resist and it is the part that works. Pretending to certainty you do not have is what invites the follow-up that breaks you. Naming the limit of your own confidence, unprompted, is the strongest available signal that the rest of what you said is solid.

## The gate

1. Does every Open tension have a prepared answer?
2. Does the obvious solution, and every rejected alternative, have a clear rebuttal with a number attached?
3. Is the single weakest point in the whole case named honestly, with a plan for it?
